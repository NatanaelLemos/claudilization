# Gauntlet loop — 2026-08-11

Running the gauntlet-loop method against Townscaper as the named reference.
One entry per improve→critique cycle: what changed / blind verdict / next
target. Verdicts are written down as they land so an aborted wave never
loses the critique.

## Wave 2, cycle 1 — the miniature grade on every desktop

- **What changed.** Shipped `ed679ae`: the photographic half of the post pass
  (split-tone, saturation, vignette, four-tap lamp glow — five taps) now runs
  on every desktop at every quality tier, and only the eight-tap tilt-shift
  defocus band stays gated to full quality. Before this, the first adaptive
  downgrade threw the entire finish away and the live world graded out flat.
- **Blind verdict.** Not yet re-run against Townscaper frames this wave: the
  wave-1 abort left the commit unvalidated and unpushed, so cycle 1 was spent
  proving it (503 tests, typecheck, production build, push, Fly v38, live
  bundle hash `index-bzEqrH5r.js` matching the local build).
- **Next target.** Water. Townscaper's sea is the strongest single element in
  its canonical frames — flat saturated field, a hard bright shoreline contact
  band, no visible tiling — and ours is the most likely blind loss.

## Standing gaps carried into wave 3

- Blind critique against real Townscaper frames has not yet been run with a
  side-by-side; the reference pass timed out this wave and must be a cheap
  local step, not a long research fan-out.
- No baked ambient occlusion. Townscaper's read is AO-dominant; ours leans on
  soft shadows plus contact blobs.
- Tilt-shift is screen-space and full-quality-desktop only, so most machines
  and every mobile frame lose the miniature cue.
- Near-shore craft still use the open-water swell amplitude.
- Existing saved buildings are reoriented and grounded but never relocated, so
  old towns read less planned than new ones.

## Wave 3, cycle 1 — per-block roof colour

- **What changed.** `9b44613` + `17aabbf` + `06338e2`: roof planes now have
  their own material identity (`roofSurface`), so `buildBuildingBatch` can
  hand every block its own deterministic roof tint through `instanceColor` —
  a street of one model is still one instanced draw, but no two roofs share a
  shade. Roof pigment keeps far more of the civ colour than the trim does
  (chalk lerp 0.48 → 0.30) with a hard saturation ceiling.
- **Blind verdict.** Two live regressions caught and fixed in-frame, both
  visible only in the game: (1) `setHSL` converts from sRGB, so the
  brightness-normalised tint multiplied every roof by ~4.7 and a whole town
  came out blown-out mint; (2) with the chalk lerp dropped, an island whose
  banner colour is already vivid rendered an electric roofline. Both now have
  unit bounds (`max channel < 1.45`, saturation capped at 0.34).
- **Next target.** The dark mass on Argyros is *not* the pitched roofs — it is
  the late-age flat parapets and district paving, which never pass through
  `roof()`. Route those through the roof material next, then re-blind.

## Wave 3, cycle 2 — the future's glow reads painted

- **What changed.** The glow pass pulls bloom 60% toward its own luminance and
  rolls it off (`bloom / (1 + bloom * 1.6)`) before adding, and hot faces bend
  toward white instead of clipping one channel. Source emissives came down
  with it: TECH 1.6 → 1.0, the holo table 1.2 → 0.6, the lab dome 0.35 → 0.28.
- **Blind verdict.** Against the Townscaper hero frame (saved this wave at
  `notes/evidence/reference/townscaper-hero.png`), Argyros' cyan panels no
  longer halo into the sky and the clipped-neon edge is gone — but they still
  own the frame's contrast. Townscaper's brightest note is a white gable, not
  a light source; ours is still the loudest object on the island.
- **Next target.** Late-age roof/paving mass (above), then water shore
  contact — the hard bright foam line and specular glint, untouched this wave.

## Wave 3, cycle 3 — the mint roofs are not the roof path

- **What changed.** Nothing in code: the live bundle `index-vRbRUfgs.js` (Fly
  v41) is byte-identical to the local build with the pigment cap in it, and
  Katsu-kaze's roofs render exactly as they did on the *pre-cap* build. The
  cap moves the roof material's own colour and changed nothing on screen.
- **Blind verdict.** So the vivid mint canopies on that island never pass
  through `roof()`/`roofMat` at all — a second colour path is dressing those
  surfaces in the island's banner hue (see `civColor.ts`, "a civ spec dressed
  in one civilization's banner color"). Against the Townscaper hero frame the
  loss is unmistakable: Townscaper's roofs are a family of warm terracotta
  shades with saturated *walls* varying block to block; ours is one flat
  fluorescent mint sheet with grey walls — the exact single-mass failure the
  wave was called to kill, just a brighter shade of it.
- **Next target.** Find the second colour path first (grep the banner-hue
  dressing through `civColor.ts` into the building materials), route it
  through the same per-block tint, then re-blind. After that: late-age flat
  parapets and district paving, then water shore contact.

## Wave 3, cycle 4 — per-block wall colour

- **What changed.** `71273e5`: wall planes get their own material identity
  (`wallSurface`, via `wallLam`), so `buildBuildingBatch` hands each block a
  deterministic wall tint through `instanceColor` alongside the roof tint —
  still one instanced draw per model part. Roof and wall tints now share one
  `blockTint(seed, hue, band)` law with named bands: roofs
  `hue 0.24 / sat .14–.28 / gain .88–1.14 / ceiling 1.45`, walls the quieter
  `hue 0.13 / sat .07–.17 / gain .93–1.08 / ceiling 1.22`.
- **Regression caught before it shipped.** Brightness normalisation divides by
  luma, and a hue with low luma (blue-leaning) pushed the dominant channel ~30%
  past the intended gain. The band now carries a hard `ceiling` and the whole
  triple is rescaled when it is exceeded — the block gets a touch darker, never
  a shifted hue. Also found the uniqueness assertion was measuring
  `getHexString()`, which clamps at 1.0 and hid real variation; it now measures
  the raw linear multiplier.
- **Blind verdict** (`evidence/2026-08-11-w3c4-walls-desktop.png` vs the
  Townscaper hero). Partial win. The town is no longer a grey extruded mass:
  cream, tan, ochre and olive walls sit under a family of warm terracotta
  roofs, and no two blocks match. But against the reference the pigment is
  still timid — Townscaper's walls are *fully* saturated pink/green/yellow/
  blue blocks, ours are sun-bleached stucco. The mosaic exists; it does not yet
  read at map height.
- **Second, larger loss now visible.** Composition. The reference frame is all
  town; ours is a small cluster in the middle of a mostly empty green island.
  That is the biggest remaining delta and it is a camera/props problem, not a
  colour one.

## Wave 3, cycle 5 — the shoreline gets a hard bright line

- **What changed.** `c5588e2`: the foam band was measured in world *height*, so
  a steep coast squeezed it to sub-pixel and a flat beach smeared it across
  half a bay. It is now widened by `fwidth(shoreDist)` — the local screen-space
  gradient — so the contact line holds one crisp width from any camera height.
  The band split into a hard `shoreCore` that reaches the foam colour outright
  (the old blend never got past 82% and read as a smudge) and a soft breathing
  `halo` behind it, plus a daylight-gated specular kick on the wet edge.
- **Blind verdict** (`evidence/2026-08-11-w3c5-shore-desktop.png`). Clear win,
  and the biggest single-frame improvement of the wave. Both the home island
  and the neighbour at the top of frame now carry a continuous bright waterline
  instead of the previous hazy turquoise halo; the coast reads as a place where
  the sea *stops*. Against the reference this is now at parity — arguably
  cleaner, since Townscaper's waterline is partly a painted texture edge.
  Watch item: our ring is very even, with the lapping variation barely visible.
- **Unit bounds added.** The shader is compiled in-test through the real
  `onBeforeCompile`, asserting the `fwidth` term is present, the specular
  coefficient stays ≤ 0.3 and daylight-gated, and the foam blend stays clamped.

## Wave 3, cycle 6 — a moored boat stops heaving

- **What changed.** `47b5579`: `scene.ts` called `water.poseAt(x, z)` with no
  depth and every craft in the world therefore rode the open-ocean swell, even
  beached in a lagoon where the GPU surface under it lies nearly flat. The
  depth field now exposes `landAt(worldX, worldZ)` — a bilinear CPU read of the
  same alpha channel the shader samples, matching its `LinearFilter` — and
  `poseAt` defaults the depth from it via the new `shoreDepth01`. No call site
  changed; every craft got the fix at once.
- **Unit bounds added.** Waterline maps to depth 0 and the deep to 1; the field
  reads land inshore and nothing 2 km out; the no-argument pose heaves less in
  the shallows than in the open, and a moored boat still breathes rather than
  freezing solid.

## Standing targets after wave 3

1. **Wall pigment is too quiet.** Cycle 4 built the mechanism and the bounds;
   the numbers are conservative. Raise the wall band toward the reference's
   painted blocks and re-blind — the ceiling and rescale make this safe now.
2. **Composition.** The town occupies a fraction of the frame the reference
   gives it. Opening camera framing and town density, not colour.
3. **Baked-AO read.** Still the deepest structural gap; cheap wins only.
4. **The plaza reads as a hole.** The grey circular paving north of the town
   is the darkest thing on the island and has no edge treatment.
