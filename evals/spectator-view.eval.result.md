# Task: Watch the world as a spectator with no account

## Actual result:

**PASS with caveats.**

- Step 1: Opened http://localhost:8787 in a completely fresh browser session.
  It loaded instantly — no signup, no login, no cookie wall. Before any island
  existed, the page was a blank dark ocean with an empty "The story so far"
  panel (defensible, since there was literally nothing in the world yet, but
  it looked broken for a first visitor).
- Step 2-3: After my island existed, every fresh visit landed the camera
  centered on Barufjord — the most recently active (and only) island, with its
  name and "Stone Age · Norse" in the corner. Never a blank ocean once an
  island existed.
- Step 4: Pan and zoom both work. Dragging orbits/tilts the camera (a lovely
  overhead view is reachable), and the scroll wheel zooms smoothly between a
  whole-world view (island a speck in a huge ocean) and a mid-range view. Zoom
  stops well short of "close-up" though — I could never get near the ground.
- Step 5: The chat panel shows messages (none ever appeared during my session)
  and there is **no input field of any kind** for a spectator — I counted zero
  visible text inputs on the whole page. Exactly as promised.

## Feeling:

Calm and pleasant. The art direction is genuinely nice — soft low-poly island,
glowing turquoise ocean when the light catches it, elegant typography. It
feels like a screensaver you'd happily leave on a wall monitor. But it's
eerily still: nothing moves, so after a minute I had nothing to watch. A
"living world" where nothing visibly lives.

## What would I change:

1. Let me zoom closer — I wanted to lean in and see daily life, and the
   camera stops at helicopter height.
2. Show something moving: settlers walking, waves, birds — anything. Stillness
   undermines the whole "watch the world live" promise.
3. When the world is empty, say so charmingly ("The ocean waits for its first
   island...") instead of showing a silent void.
